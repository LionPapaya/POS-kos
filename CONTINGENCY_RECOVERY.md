# Four-RAPIER-loss contingency recovery

The takeoff GUI V1 estimate is display-only and does not replace the live runway-stop check that selects runway abort or RTLS before this contingency executive is entered. Its runway, mass, and calibration limits are documented in [README](README.md#takeoff-gui-v1-estimate).

The ascent and active RTLS/ATR recovery loops execute `Libraries/Poseidon_SSTO/contingency.ks` when the sticky RAPIER loss count reaches the existing contingency threshold. Failure refresh does not reset an ongoing contingency phase. The executive disables warp, cuts the RAPIERs, and activates healthy NERVs for airborne recovery even when they were previously off. Failed NERV IDs remain blocked. On Kerbin, contingency retains automatic control through failed plans and degraded approaches. It finishes after stopped ground contact (ground speed below 1 m/s), actual water contact, or a bound atmosphere-clear orbit; commanded and initial pilot throttle are zero at completion. Ground stop and committed ocean ditching shut down NERVs. This change adds no recovery support for other bodies; the existing unsupported-body guard remains.

If the NERVs were already active when the contingency was detected, recovery first tries to reach a safe bound orbit. Otherwise, or after insertion is rejected, priority is a reachable runway entry/runway, then a sampled ocean corridor, then sampled flat land. Runway candidates pay an altitude/kinetic-energy budget, a turn allowance, and a 35% reserve using an initial conservative glide ratio of 4. Candidates behind the aircraft, approaches requiring excessive alignment or descent, and sampled obstructing ridges are rejected. Terrain landing corridors are 1,800 m long and 200 m wide, with at most 8 m of sampled relief. These are coarse screening criteria, not obstacle or slope certification. Selection is bounded in live flight time, rechecks live clearance, and refreshes DAP between candidates. Failure of this screened search switches to emergency landing selection; it does not release controls.

Ground failures command braking until actual stopped ground contact. An orbit attempt aims initially for 100 km apoapsis and a periapsis 10 km above the atmosphere, with 100 m insertion tolerance. It targets a steep 60 degree pitch during apoapsis raise, but in the atmosphere limits the initial pull-up to 15 degrees above the current flight path and avoids raising the nose when below approach speed. It then coasts and performs an alignment-gated, tapered tangential insertion burn. Available current-pressure thrust, estimated vacuum delta-v, fuel, a 200 m/s loss allowance, actual apoapsis/periapsis growth, descending clearance, and a 480-second timeout can reject the attempt. This fuel/energy screen is an optimistic lower bound permitting a monitored attempt, not a guaranteed ascent prediction.

The controller initially sets aside 10% of the starting liquid fuel. It releases that reserve when the orbit budget otherwise fails, normal usable fuel is exhausted while airborne, or emergency landing recovery is required. Reserve release is logged once and power/energy estimates are refreshed; healthy-engine isolation and survivor thrust limits still apply. Actual fuel exhaustion ends thrust, not automatic steering. It estimates recovery delta-v from current ISP and liquid-fuel mass, and uses the surviving engines' vacuum ISP for the orbit-attempt fuel screen so atmospheric inefficiency does not rule out the coming vacuum burn; actual thrust availability and progress checks catch flameout or inaccessible fuel. Recovery energy screens credit at most a derated 20-second NERV burn, capped at 40 m/s added speed. Throttle uses the unpowered energy deficit so crediting future thrust cannot falsely suppress the burn needed to deliver it. Normal atmospheric steering protection stays enabled, while coast/insertion timing owns the throttle command. The current-pressure and vacuum engine readings follow the [kOS engine suffix definitions](https://ksp-kos.github.io/KOS/structures/vessels/engine.html).

After insertion rejection, high-altitude/high-speed recovery tries an upstream runway entry point initially 20 km before the threshold and 10 km above it. It banks toward that entry and requests NERV thrust when speed or energy is deficient. This is a powered geometric/energy screen, not a solved hypersonic trajectory. If no entry passes the screen, it retains wings-level AoA control until the glide envelope is reached and searches the landing alternatives. A safe bound orbit completes an eligible orbit attempt with the existing 10 km atmospheric margin and 100 m tolerance. An already-bound orbit with periapsis at or above the atmosphere is preserved when no insertion attempt is eligible or an attempt was abandoned. The executive cuts thrust and finishes successfully without a deorbit burn. Escape trajectories are not counted as stable orbits. Insufficient search height, missing screened corridors, unsafe final capture/speed/bank/alignment, and recovery timeouts retain automatic control on Kerbin. The generic ascent/recovery cleanup does not reacquire throttle, change gear, or reactivate engines after completion.

When the screened options fail, `emergency_glide` chooses the best available sampled option even if none meets normal corridor criteria. Its survival score penalizes intervening terrain rise, negative energy margin, sampled relief, required turn, distance, and mixed coast/water samples; uniform water is preferred only when the terrain/energy penalties permit it. The scan looks ahead within a bounded range, uses at most a one-second search budget (shorter when descending rapidly), and yields to DAP between candidates. These weights are initial heuristics, not a survival probability. A lost emergency target may be reselected at five-second intervals while sufficient height remains. Targets behind the aircraft do not command a turnback.

Below 150 m local clearance, the executive skips the terrain search and selects the local surface along the current track. An uncaptured or unsafe normal final also switches to this local landing attempt. A low abort that is still climbing remains in `emergency_glide`, preserving normal terrain protection; low height alone no longer commits the aircraft to final and suppresses GPWS. The descending final retains its ownership through subsequent bounces. `emergency_final` commands wings level, a rate-limited AoA to reduce sink, and speed-based NERV assistance until 15 m lowest-point clearance above terrain or water. Low-speed land recovery reduces its AoA limit only after excessive sink has been arrested; crossing 132 m/s while sinking no longer removes lift. Land airbrakes require a speed margin and both current and projected sink within the requested descent band; gear is up over water and down over land below 500 m clearance. Landing AoA, rate, power cutoff, search weights and relief margins require KSP/FAR calibration. The actual local water surface is checked on every emergency update, irrespective of the original target type. A land target crossing the coast changes to local water approach at the full water-entry height, with its position and altitude refreshed; it no longer waits until 15 m. If actual low-altitude flight reaches deep water, it switches to the committed ditch controller; if a ditch reaches land or shallow water, it changes to local emergency touchdown. Contact bounces retain terminal protection ownership.

The 1,200-second recovery limit is now a strategy deadline. An expired runway-entry plan is rejected in favor of wings-level atmospheric coast; adequate-speed coast commands zero thrust instead of spending fuel against an arbitrary range deficit. An expired glide switches to emergency landing. Ground stopping continues braking and committed ditching continues clearance/AoA control through timeouts. Each degraded reason is logged once; there is no elapsed-time control release on Kerbin.

Automatic runway/land recovery uses NERV assistance during the glide when needed, cuts commanded thrust for the final flare, retains landing steering through brief ground-contact bounces, and completes only after stopped ground contact. Water recovery keeps the gear up. Over a captured water corridor, or a local emergency water surface, `ocean_approach` takes ownership at 400 m lowest-point clearance or ten seconds of downward travel, whichever gives more height. It preserves the actual/smoothed pull-up command, including GPWS steering and its AoA smoother, then uses wings-level AoA control with the existing 25 degree water limit during both approach and hold. It approaches a 3 m hull-clearance hold at at most 2 m/s requested sink. Height feedback uses current hull clearance plus three seconds of current vertical travel and the measured constant-acceleration contribution, so it starts flattening the descent before reaching the target instead of waiting for height error after overshooting it. Once below the hold-entry gate (15 m plus up to three seconds of the bounded approach sink, at most 21 m), it latches `ocean_ditch`, cuts NERVs and continues regulating the 3 m target unpowered. Climbing above the gate does not restart propulsion. A steep descent does not expand the power-cut gate beyond 21 m.

Before commitment, airbrakes require speed above 152 m/s and both current and projected sink within the requested descent band. This retains the existing 20 m/s lift-energy reserve during approach. NERVs assist below the 132 m/s approach reference; if speed is below 152 m/s and projected sink exceeds the request, assistance also responds to that sink deficit, reaching full throttle for a deficit of 2 m/s. Fast descending approaches receive no added thrust from this sink term. During the unpowered hold, brakes operate while sink is controlled and release if current or projected sink becomes excessive. No speed threshold lowers the water AoA command while descending too fast. Measured changes in vertical speed give a three-second response lead: the controller begins restoring lift before a fading climb becomes a steep descent, and backs off a pull-up when measured upward acceleration is already arresting sink. Stale/sub-tick differentiation returns zero. These initial control margins require KSP validation, not a certified trajectory prediction.

As speed decays the unpowered hold increases AoA up to 25 degrees and permits natural loss of lift and settling. It commands neither continued descent through the hold height nor forced contact at a particular speed. GPWS, automatic throttle recovery and the normal low-speed AoA cap cannot take over this committed ditch. Actual `SPLASHED` status records `ocean_contact`; this confirms water contact, not structural survival or safe impact loads.

The settings live in `contingency_config()`. The initial glide, speed, bank, hold height, AoA, and controller gains require KSP/FAR calibration. Normal protection remains active during ordinary and emergency glide. Normal final, water approach, committed ditching, and emergency final own terminal protection through contact and bounces. No normal stall-speed dive command is applied during the committed water hold. The user expects settling near 110 m/s; this is logged as a reference, not a forced-contact or pitch-down speed. The 400 m/ten-second entry lead, clearance-response lead and approach limits still need KSP/FAR validation.

Atmospheric landing recovery enables the existing FTE-1 vessel fuel drain down to a fixed 10% LF floor measured at the start of that atmospheric recovery. The valve drains its configured resources; the remaining NERVs need only LF. Flight_342 showed LF draining at about 40 units/s with oxidizer unchanged, so the current valve configuration must not be treated as an instant reduction to the LF floor or proof that oxidizer is being dumped. The old protected engine reserve is released so the retained LF can be spent if necessary. The floor never shrinks on subsequent ticks. Dumping is disabled for orbit rescue/preservation and stopped on completion; drain lag can overshoot the floor and must be checked in KSP. Fuel flow and mass are logged rather than treated as instantaneous mass changes.

## Evidence and logger

`tests/test_contingency.py` executes the actual source and checks the independent work/energy calculation, corridor relief and ridge rejection, runway/ocean/land priority, phase persistence, live search timing, automatic emergency fallback, terminal control retention, safe completion, reserve release, flare/ditch commands, protection ownership, and logging cadence. Pure controller mathematics also execute through `tools/pos_sim/kos_runtime.py` at 2,000 IPU and 0.02-second ticks. `tools/pos_sim/contingency_scenarios.py` also runs the actual controller against independent central-gravity physics, fuel consumption, and a slew-limited attitude actuator. The nominal vacuum fixture uses two stock 60 kN NERVs, a 50-tonne initial mass, 800 s vacuum ISP, and 3,000 liquid-fuel units. It raises apoapsis, coasts, and inserts into a safe bound orbit; low-fuel and low-thrust fixtures reject insertion. Reports and traces belong under the ignored `tools/pos_sim/results/` directory. The fixture uses 2,000 IPU and 0.02-second physics ticks, with logging virtualized as a passive observer. It does not model atmospheric flight or validate runway entry, aerodynamic pull-up, landing, or water impact.

Low logging records escalation, NERV policy/availability, orbit attempt/rejection, reserve release, runway entry/rejection, phase changes, screened/emergency landing targets, one-time degraded reasons, fuel-dump transitions, early water capture, unpowered hold commitment, precontact evidence, and final outcome. Medium logging adds cached decision and vessel observations once per second; high uses the existing tick cadence. Samples include range, energy margin, engine loss counts, fuel, mass, speed, vertical speed, throttle, GPWS state, NERV available thrust/acceleration/ISP/delta-v, fuel reserve, powered versus unpowered energy margin, orbit budget and apsides, insertion alignment/pitch, runway entry range/margin, ditch AoA/lowest-point altitude, emergency AoA/requested sink, lowest-point clearance above local terrain, cached target risk/relief/ridge/water fraction, replan time, last degraded reason, commanded versus actual throttle/AoA, water sink target/brakes/power/entry height, the fixed fuel-dump floor, cached raw local terrain height, measured vertical acceleration and projected sink, the water-clearance target/projected clearance/hold gate, AoA saturation and command-versus-actual AoA error. Synthetic lagged-lift tests exercise the actual KerboScript controller with an independent linear lift/actuator plant at 2,000 IPU and 0.02 s ticks, including 120-second low-altitude holds with 0.5–2 s actuator lag. These are control-stability checks, not FAR or stall calibration. First hull contact freezes the previous airborne speed, sink, mass, AoA and command before water drag or part loss can hide the impact; a touchdown summary carries those values even when post-impact speed is near zero. Logging performs no terrain search or trajectory prediction and never influences guidance.

## KSP/FAR flight validation still required

Use a recoverable save and the actual Poseidon configuration. Capture medium/high flight logs for each case, including the failure instant and final outcome. Keep unsuccessful runs for comparison; do not declare this flight-ready from the offline checks alone.

| Case | Expected evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Four losses during runway roll | `ground_stop`, zero thrust, brakes, real stopped contact; no false airborne completion |
| Four losses with a reachable runway ahead | Runway selected before alternatives; conservative margin; aligned final; stopped ground contact |
| No reachable runway over open ocean | Ocean selected before land; gear up; early `ocean_approach` followed by `ocean_ditch`; lowest-point clearance near 3 m while slowing; AoA rises; water contact speed and sink logged |
| Water hold below normal stall speed | No GPWS pull-up, automatic throttle floor, or low-speed AoA clamp; pitch authority retained until contact |
| Runways and water unreachable, nearby flat land | Sampled land selected; corridor capture; descending flare and ground stop |
| Coastal/rough terrain candidates | Screened candidates rejected; emergency options ranked with explicit terrain/energy risk; control retained |
| High-energy suborbital coast | Entry AoA control, no warp, atmospheric fuel dumping to the fixed LF floor after orbit rescue is rejected; glide-envelope transition; expired entry plans continue controlled coast without automatic fuel waste |
| Four losses after NERV activation with adequate fuel/thrust | `orbit_raise` → `orbit_coast` → `orbit_insert` → `nerv_orbit_complete`; atmosphere-safe periapsis and preserved reserve |
| Orbit attempt stops making progress / loses NERV thrust | Logged rejection; no repeated orbit attempt; powered runway entry attempted before landing alternatives |
| Early failure before NERV activation | Healthy NERVs activated for recovery; no orbit-first attempt; measurable assistance when energy/speed requires it |
| Stable orbit | Eligible attempt completes; otherwise preserved with zero thrust and successful completion; no automatic deorbit |
| Coast / insertion alignment | Zero coast throttle; no atmospheric throttle floor; insertion burns only while aligned |
| Fuel reserve needed | Reserve released when needed, estimates refreshed, healthy NERVs remain usable until actual exhaustion |
| Further losses during RTLS/ATR or rejected runway stop | Escalation enters the same executive; phase persists; cleanup preserves the final recovery outcome |
| Unsafe/uncaptured final, or exhausted search height | Emergency landing ahead, wings-level terminal commands, retained control until contact |

Also validate excess-speed/bank final, heading mismatch, low-speed final, missed corridor, elevated terrain, land-to-water and water-to-land transitions, normal/emergency target loss, each timeout, and wheel-contact bounces. These cases must retain controls with the logged reason and complete only after the agreed safe/contact outcomes.

Inspect phase order, first rejection/failure, attitude/bank, commanded versus actual AoA, control authority, fuel, lowest-point clearance, contact speed, vertical speed, and any vehicle damage. The offline model lacks aerodynamic moments, engine gimbal, flex, RCS authority, slopes, gear suspension, bounce, wheel friction, and water-impact behavior; those margins need actual KSP/FAR flights.
